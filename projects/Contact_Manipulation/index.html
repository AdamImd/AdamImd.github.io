A touch on the arm changes the problem. Contact IK puts that information inside the inverse-kinematics objective, so the robot can adjust its posture while pursuing a hand goal.

The idea

Give contact a place in the objective.

01

Locate the touch

Express an observed contact point in the robot's world frame. Link geometry tells the solver how sampled surface points move with each joint.

02

Combine the objectives

Keep the hand-pose goal, add a penalty for sampled points near the contact input, and include joint-limit terms.

03

Update the posture

Differentiate through forward kinematics and update the joints. The contact term can change the solution across the arm.

A closer look at the contact term

The illustrated formulation sums squared penalties for sampled points within an 8 cm radius of a fixed observed contact point. The samples form a synthetic geometric atlas. The cost is an optimization signal; it is not a pressure measurement or a guarantee of collision-free motion.

Both rendered conditions project each optimizer update to the same joint bounds. This explicit visualization modification keeps the displayed configurations bounded. The original study below used the unprojected formulation with a final command-boundary projection.

Results & context

What the original study showed

The original formulation study evaluated 28 recorded Franka states, including 12 with contact. The contact term reduced the avoidance residual in every contact-positive case. Pose accuracy, solve speed, and reliable convergence remained limiting.

Where the research goes next

My broader work connects contact sensing to manipulation along the arm and body. The first hardware direction is the Spot arm with the existing force-sensing skins, using bounded, supervised experiments. Related simulation studies explore pushing, bracing, and pivoting.

ContactIK-MPC is a separate research extension. It adds learned objectives and short-horizon contact prediction. The videos on this page demonstrate the original geometric idea and its joint-bounded visualization variant.

Related whole-body simulation

A planar pushing and bracing setup helped diagnose whole-body contact tasks. It is a separate simulation study, with learned-policy and hardware validation still open.
